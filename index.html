Vision

Establish one intelligent, governed Strategic Network Planning Object

SNIP will realise this vision by creating a persistent, intelligent and governed Strategic Network Planning Object. It combines internal performance and planning evidence with governed external intelligence to provide a complete strategic perspective, retain the rationale and history behind material decisions, connect technical ambition with financial commitment and delivery, and generate explainable insight for earlier intervention and continuous improvement.

Why this matters

Vodafone’s network planning cycle influences €[X] billion of investment and involves ~300 stakeholders, yet there is no single governed record linking ambition and rationale to expected cost, approval, funding, delivery and realised outcomes. Evidence is distributed across stages, platforms and manually assembled records — this limits traceability and makes it hard to explain whether investment delivered the intended outcome.

What will change

One persistent planning identity across the lifecycle, governed KPI definitions, source validation, version history, traceability from strategy to outcome, self-service analysis, and progressively stronger forecasting and decision support — delivered in controlled phases. Platform replacement is a consequence of the target operating model, not the starting point.

1. What SNIP Is

Strategic Network Intelligent Planning

Its purpose is to establish one intelligent, governed Strategic Network Planning Object that fuses the internal lens — objectives, KPIs, targets, investment commitments, delivery and outcomes — with the external lens of competitor activity, market dynamics and emerging technologies. Today, each stage of the planning cycle contains part of the truth; SNIP creates continuity across those stages by maintaining a persistent planning identity and the evidence associated with each material decision.

1.1 The SNIP plan object
NEP→ Programme→ Technical KPI & target→ SNIP planning record→ LRP commitment→ DB commitment→ CAR decision→ SAP WBS→ SNIP delivery mgmt→ Actual performance→ Realised outcome

The plan object should retain:

  • Market, planning period and plan version
  • Strategic objective, network domain, vertical and programme
  • KPI definition, baseline and target
  • Assumptions and dependencies
  • Planning identifier, financial commitment and funding status
  • WBS, delivery status and actual performance
  • Variance, supporting evidence and decision history
  • Accountable owner and approver
1.2 What SNIP is not
  • A standalone replacement for only one platform
  • An AI chatbot placed over existing data
  • A simple migration of OHP, BPT or PPM without redesigning the end-to-end planning process
  • A programme to replace every enterprise system that supplies data to network planning
  • An autonomous decision-maker
1.3 The intended outcome
  • Preserve the reasoning behind planning decisions
  • Connect technical ambition with financial commitment
  • Validate information before it enters the cycle
  • Understand how the plan changes over time
  • Identify emerging delivery or performance risk
  • Compare outcomes consistently
  • Evaluate whether investment delivered the intended result
  • Improve future planning using evidence from previous cycles
2. Why Now

2.1 Network planning decisions require broader evidence

Network planning increasingly needs to balance network performance, customer demand, capacity requirements, investment constraints, market priorities, delivery capability, technology evolution, competitor position and changing commercial performance — to know whether performance diverged because of financial, operational or external causes, and which intervention is most appropriate.

2.2 Enabling data and analytical capabilities are becoming available

Vodafone has an opportunity to combine strategic planning information, governed KPI history, financial commitments, funding decisions, programme delivery information, network actuals, market benchmarking, competitor context, forecasting and explainable decision support. These capabilities cannot generate reliable value when built on disconnected identifiers, inconsistent definitions or uncontrolled planning history — the immediate priority is to establish the common foundation once and let subsequent capabilities reuse it.

2.3 The current planning foundation must change

Vodafone can either address individual technical limitations while maintaining the existing planning model, or use the required change to establish one governed capability across the planning cycle. The first approach restores individual components. The second improves how Vodafone plans, funds, monitors and learns from network investment.

2.4 Delay weakens future intelligence

  • Assumptions are not consistently retained
  • Corrections occur outside the authoritative source
  • Target definitions may drift
  • Technical and financial records remain difficult to connect
  • The reason behind changes may not be preserved
  • Decisions continue to depend on manually reconstructed evidence
The longer Vodafone waits to create governed planning history, the longer it postpones the intelligence that depends on that history.

6. What Prevents the Change Today

6.1 No single plan object

Technical planning, financial commitment and execution status are distributed across OHP, BPT and PPM, while actual spend remains in SAP. SNIP will replace OHP, BPT and PPM with one persistent planning record that maintains identity, mapping and decision history through to the SAP WBS.

6.2 Limited process-floor integration

Key planning information leaves existing platforms through spreadsheet extracts. This prevents reliable workflow automation and constrains controlled use of forecasting or analytical models.

6.3 No governed external dimension

Competitor and market context are not systematically connected to the planning record.

6.4 Insufficiently governed history

KPI naming, calculation logic, vertical structures and classifications have changed over time, weakening comparability across markets, versions and periods.

6.5 Fragile operational components

OHP, BPT and PPM each hold important parts of the current process, but their fragmented architecture prevents a single governed record. SNIP will replace all three and consolidate their relevant capabilities within one supported product.

7. What Intelligent Planning Means

7.1 Explain — why did the gap open?

Distinguish delivery shortfall, timing, phasing, assumption error, funding change, target or baseline revision, data quality, structural trend and external cause.

7.2 Anticipate — will the target still be met?

Estimate expected landing, confidence range, emerging risk and the assumptions influencing the forecast. Production use follows validation against an agreed baseline.

7.3 Compare — is Vodafone falling behind, or is the market changing?

Support cross-market benchmarking using conformed KPI definitions and external context where governed data is available.

7.4 Recommend — what action should be taken?

Propose evidence-based actions through the correct planning route. A named planner must accept, reject, amend or defer the recommendation, with rationale retained.

SNIP cannot become intelligent until the planning cycle becomes governable. Intelligence is the final layer, not the first.

9.3 – 9.4 Efficiency hypothesis & classification discipline

The efficiency figure is a working hypothesis pending process-owner and Finance validation. The baseline must identify activity, role, location, frequency, volume, average duration, automation potential, residual activity and signed approval.

  • Cashable: a budget, external service, overtime cost or role cost is genuinely removed.
  • Capacity released: colleagues redirect time to modelling, analysis, intervention or governance.
  • Avoided cost: a future expense or duplicated transformation is prevented.

9.5 Strategic value

Decision-quality value should initially be presented as upside. A defensible calculation requires capital genuinely in scope, a specific decision SNIP can influence, an observable improvement measure, a counterfactual baseline, attribution, confidence and protection against double counting.

The initial case should stand on control, continuity, validated efficiency and avoided duplication. Decision-quality value is additional upside.

Closing Recommendation

Vodafone’s network planning cycle makes strategic, financial and delivery decisions across NEP, LRP, DB, CAR and monthly performance management. The process is well established, but its underlying evidence remains fragmented across stages, identities and systems. SNIP addresses this structural gap by creating one governed planning capability. Phase 1 delivers immediate value through controlled intake, validation at source, consistent KPI definitions, governed history, reduced manual assembly and improved self-service.

Later phases build on that foundation to connect cost, funding, delivery and realised outcomes, while introducing forecasting, benchmarking, variance explanation and evidence-based recommendations. Intelligence must be earned through governed data, demonstrated accuracy and human accountability.

Approve Phase 1 of SNIP to establish the governed planning foundation connecting Vodafone’s network ambition, investment decisions and realised outcomes.
